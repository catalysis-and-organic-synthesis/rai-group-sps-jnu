Designing sustainable catalysts for selective synthesis

We build metal-free, recyclable catalysts, chiefly modified graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4), and use light, organocatalysis and solid-base chemistry to control which bond forms, where it forms and which enantiomer is made.

Visible-light photocatalysis

Oxidised and engineered carbon nitrides run C–C, C–N, C–O and C–halogen bond-forming reactions under household LEDs.

Asymmetric catalysis

Heterogeneous photocatalysts paired with chiral organocatalysts give congested quaternary stereocentres in high enantiomeric excess.

Divergent synthesis

One catalyst, one feedstock, different products: temperature, substituents and added reagents steer selectivity.

Graphene oxide carbocatalysis

Metal-free GO and plant-extract-reduced GO catalyse C–H activation, aziridination and reductions in water.

Stereoselective heterocycles

Organocatalytic and multicomponent cascades give azetidines, thietanes, β-lactams, piperidines and sugar-fused pyrimidines.

Electrochemical sensors

Graphene and carbon nitride nanocomposite electrodes detect pollutants, drugs and industrial chemicals.

    Research

    What we work on

    Over nearly two decades the group's work has moved from stereoselective organocatalysis and multicomponent heterocycle synthesis to metal-free, recyclable carbon catalysts: graphitic carbon nitride and graphene oxide. We tune their surfaces to control selectivity, and we also use them to build electrochemical sensors.

    01CNO · hν
    PhotocatalysisMaterials

    Engineered graphitic carbon nitride catalysts

    We treat g-C3N4 with acid to make oxidised carbon nitride (CNO / Ox-CN), adding carbonyl and carboxyl groups, or load it with alkali to make NaOH-Ox-CN. We follow the surface chemistry by PXRD, FTIR, XPS, SEM/TEM and EPR, and link those changes to catalytic selectivity.

    • Regioselective ring opening of N-tosylaziridines and epoxides under blue light, with 86–95% yield, gram scale and 5 reuse cycles
    • Single-electron-transfer pathways supported by radical trapping (TEMPO, BHT) and DPPH-EPR studies
    • Metal-doped carbon nitrides (Cu@g-C3N4, Co@g-C3N4, Cu/Cu2O@g-C3N4) as visible-light photocatalysts for nitriles, γ-ketonitriles, 1H-tetrazoles, benzimidazoles and Hantzsch/Biginelli heterocycles, often in water
    02¹O₂ + L-proline
    AsymmetricDual catalysis

    Enantioselective heterogeneous photocatalysis

    CNO generates singlet oxygen under light while L-proline supplies a chiral enamine, and the two cycles work together to build C2-quaternary indolin-3-ones. This is a rare case of enantioselective photocatalysis with carbon nitride.

    • 94–99% ee, and switching to D-proline gives the opposite enantiomer
    • Extended to enantiodivergent α-C(sp3)–H functionalisation of N-aryl glycine esters
    03 GO · rGO
    CarbocatalysisGreen chemistry

    Graphene oxide carbocatalysis and bio-derived materials

    Graphene oxide (GO) works as a metal-free catalyst in water. It drives C–H activation to amines and amides, nitrene insertion into olefins to give aziridines, hydride-transfer reductions, thiazolidinone cascades and phase-transfer reactions. The group also prepares reduced GO from plant extracts (tulsi, neem, datura, citrus) and makes GO rapidly by microwave-assisted oxidation, then uses these materials both in catalysis and in sensors.

    • Metal-free C–H activation over GO toward amines and amides in water (J. Org. Chem. 2020)
    • Bio-reduced GO for nitro-group reduction, oxime-to-amine reduction and epoxide ring opening
    04NaOH-Ox-CN
    SelectivityMechanism

    Divergent, ring-opening and reagent-controlled synthesis

    Ring-opening–ring-closing strategies turn aziridines and epoxides into aza- and oxa-heterocycles. NaOH-Ox-CN works as a recyclable solid base. It takes salicylaldehydes and 1,3-diketones down to either hemiketals or 9-substituted tetrahydroxanthenones, and we study the branch point with Hammett analysis, crossover experiments and X-ray crystallography. Pristine g-C3N4 converts styrenes into 4-aryl-α-tetralones (with Katritzky salts as non-incorporating electron acceptors) or into α-iodoketones (with I2 and air).

    05 Baylis–Hillman · NHC
    OrganocatalysisHeterocycles

    Organocatalysis and stereoselective heterocycle synthesis

    The group's roots are in stereoselective synthetic methodology. Morita–Baylis–Hillman adducts, nitroalkenes, NHC and amine organocatalysis, ionic liquids and carbohydrates as bio-renewable building blocks give multicomponent and cascade routes to strained and polyfunctional heterocycles.

    • 3-Nitroazetidines, 3-nitrothietanes, 1,3-thiazetidines, β-sultams and β-lactams
    • Piperidines, tetrahydropyridines and decahydroquinolines by NHC and iminium–enamine cascades
    • Sugar-annulated pyrimidines and pyridines from carbohydrates via Biginelli and ring-transformation chemistry
    06 Electrochemical sensing
    CollaborativeSensors

    Nanocomposites for electrochemical sensing

    In a long-running collaboration, we design graphene, reduced-GO, g-C3N4 and metal-nanoparticle composites as modified electrodes for sensitive electrochemical detection.

    • Analytes include hydrazine, cadmium, organophosphate pesticides, p-phenylenediamine, p-aminophenol, acetaminophen, quinol, nitrobenzene, p-nitrophenol, ascorbic acid and p-chloro-m-xylenol
    • Published in Sens. Actuators B, Talanta, Electroanalysis, J. Electroanal. Chem. and other journals, along with reviews and book chapters

    Principal Investigator

    Dr. Ankita Rai

    Assistant Professor, School of Physical Sciences, Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi

    Dr. Rai's group works on sustainable synthetic methodology. Its work spans organocatalysis and stereoselective heterocycle synthesis, carbocatalysis with graphene oxide, and metal-free heterogeneous photocatalysis with engineered graphitic carbon nitride, along with nanocomposite electrochemical sensors. The group values rigorous mechanistic work, with control experiments and reproducible results at the centre of every project.
