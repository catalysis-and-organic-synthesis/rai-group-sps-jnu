Designing sustainable catalysts for selective organic synthesis

We build metal-free, recyclable catalysts, chiefly modified graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4), and use light, organocatalysis and solid-base chemistry to control which bond forms, where it forms and which enantiomer is made.

    Research

    What we work on

    Our central idea is that carbon nitride is more than a simple photosensitiser. It is a scaffold we can tune. Oxidation, alkali loading or a change of added reagent turns it into a set of catalysts, each built for a particular selectivity problem.

    PhotocatalysisMaterials

    Engineered graphitic carbon nitride catalysts

    We treat g-C3N4 with acid to make oxidised carbon nitride (CNO / Ox-CN), adding carbonyl and carboxyl groups, or load it with alkali to make NaOH-Ox-CN. We follow the surface chemistry by PXRD, FTIR, XPS, SEM/TEM and EPR, and link those changes to catalytic selectivity.

    • Regioselective ring opening of N-tosylaziridines and epoxides under blue light, with 86–95% yield, gram scale and 5 reuse cycles
    • Single-electron-transfer pathways supported by radical trapping (TEMPO, BHT) and DPPH-EPR studies
    AsymmetricDual catalysis

    Enantioselective heterogeneous photocatalysis

    CNO generates singlet oxygen under light while L-proline supplies a chiral enamine, and the two cycles work together to build C2-quaternary indolin-3-ones. This is a rare case of enantioselective photocatalysis with carbon nitride.

    • 94–99% ee, and switching to D-proline gives the opposite enantiomer
    • Extended to enantiodivergent α-C(sp3)–H functionalisation of N-aryl glycine esters
    SelectivityMechanism

    Divergent and reagent-controlled synthesis

    NaOH-Ox-CN works as a recyclable solid base. It takes salicylaldehydes and 1,3-diketones down to either hemiketals or 9-substituted tetrahydroxanthenones, and we study the branch point with Hammett analysis, crossover experiments and X-ray crystallography. Pristine g-C3N4 converts styrenes into 4-aryl-α-tetralones (with Katritzky salts as non-incorporating electron acceptors) or into α-iodoketones (with I2 and air).

    OrganocatalysisHeterocycles

    Organocatalysis and stereoselective heterocycle synthesis

    The group started out in synthetic organic chemistry, using Baylis–Hillman adducts, nitroalkenes, NHC catalysis, ionic liquids and bio-renewable resources for stereoselective, multicomponent routes to small heterocycles such as 3-nitroazetidines, 3-nitrothietanes, β-sultams and N-formyl piperidines.

    CollaborativeSensors

    Nanocomposites for electrochemical sensing

    With collaborators, we build graphene-oxide and nanoalloy composites for sensitive electrochemical detection of hydrazine, cadmium and p-phenylenediamine.

    Principal Investigator

    Dr. Ankita Rai

    Assistant Professor, School of Physical Sciences, Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi

    Dr. Rai's group works on sustainable synthetic methodology: organocatalysis, stereoselective heterocycle synthesis and, more recently, metal-free heterogeneous photocatalysis with engineered graphitic carbon nitride. The group values rigorous mechanistic work, with control experiments and reproducible results at the centre of every project.
